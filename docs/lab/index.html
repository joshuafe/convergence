A LITTLE SCIENCE. A LITTLE WORDPLAY.

Learn a language.
Make it do something.

  1. Inspect your private observation. Compare the before and after pictures. Which token changed? What could the symbol mean?
  2. Give symbols your own word clues. Use a nickname such as “pocket” or “lookout” in your working dictionary. Select a proposed meaning to make your theory explicit.
  3. Compare with Nori. Your computer partner has a different observation. Ask for a clue, a useful test, or its original note. Share your note whenever helpful.
  4. Build and run messages. Choose two tokens and a symbol. Add steps to try a sequence. Watch the actual scene change. Invalid instructions explain what prevented a move.
  5. Complete the construction. Satisfy every mission condition, then check the scene. Any arrangement or message that meets the conditions is accepted.

No timer, scores, or guess penalties. Reset the scene and keep what you learned. Every dialect uses each of its three actions exactly once, and the same object order for every symbol.

About this solo mockup

Nori is a rule-based partner. It reasons from its own observation and experiments shared in the notebook. It cannot see your private note until you share it. Your word clues are labels; the selected meaning communicates the hypothesis. Nori does not interpret unrestricted chat.

This is a local solo simulation, with three introductory missions and remix variations. Progress stays in this browser. No account, external AI, or multiplayer connection is used.
